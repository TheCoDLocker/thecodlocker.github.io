Should You Copy a Pro’s Settings?

Copying a pro’s settings is a reasonable way to get started. The trouble comes when you keep switching players’ setups after every bad game. You never get much time to learn how any of them feels.

What the pro numbers tell you

Most confirmed BO7 profiles on CODLOCKER use sensitivity between 1.65 and 1.85 and FOV between 98 and 105. Dynamic and Default are the usual response-curve and aim-assist choices. If you’re starting from scratch, that’s a useful set of numbers to work from.

It shows what’s common in this group of players. It doesn’t prove that everything outside the range is wrong, or that one number inside it will make you more accurate.

What they can’t tell you

A settings page can’t account for your thumbsticks, how you hold the controller, your screen or what feels comfortable to you. Deadzones are especially dependent on the pad in your hands.

Even teammates use different sensitivities, FOVs and button layouts. There’s enough variation that you shouldn’t feel you’ve missed a secret setting because your setup looks a little different.

Give yourself a fair comparison

Choose a setup close to what you already use, or start around the middle of the recorded range. Play a few sessions and pay attention to a specific problem: aiming past targets, struggling to track at range or finding it hard to turn in close fights.

Change the setting that relates to that problem, in a small step. Keep the others the same. Changing your sensitivity, curve and FOV together makes it hard to know which change helped.

Once your aim feels predictable and the controls are comfortable, leave them alone for a while. You can revisit them when there’s a clear reason, such as a new controller or a change in the game.
