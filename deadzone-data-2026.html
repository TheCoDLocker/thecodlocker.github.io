CDL Deadzones: Where the Numbers Differ

Left 1/75 and right 3/99 are common, but they don’t describe every setup. Here’s the spread across 45 recorded BO7 players.

You’ll see left 1/75 and right 3/99 repeated on plenty of pro settings pages. It’s a useful reference, but the full records show where players choose something different.

The right-stick maximum is the most consistent value in this snapshot: all 45 confirmed entries use 99. The other three values have more variation.

Left-stick minimum

A minimum of 1 is the most common left-stick value, appearing in 32 of the 45 records. Ten players use 0, including Mercules, Shotzzy, aBeZy, HyDra, Neptune, 04, Simp, Exnid, Nastie and Lunarz.

A zero removes the configured inner deadzone. Whether that’s comfortable depends on the controller. The number alone doesn’t tell us the condition of the sticks or why a player chose it.

Left-stick maximum

Most entries use 75, but the recorded values extend lower. Alluka uses 60, Mamba uses 63, and Sib and Nium use 65. A value of 70 appears for aBeZy, Exnid, Kips and Lunarz.

A lower maximum lets the game register full movement input before the stick reaches the edge. Ghosty sits at the other end with 99, the only recorded left maximum that high in this set.

Right-stick minimum

The right minimum clusters around 3. The lower end includes Alluka and Kremp at 1; the highest recorded value is Mamba at 6. These are recorded choices, not enough evidence to diagnose drift on someone else’s controller.

What to try on your own pad

Use the pro numbers as a reference, then test both sticks yourself. If the camera or character moves when you let go, raise the relevant minimum in small steps. Adjust the left maximum separately if you want full movement input to register with less travel.

The deadzone guide walks through that process. You can compare the wider set of recorded settings on the league stats page.
