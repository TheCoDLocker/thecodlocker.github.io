The Deadzone Data: What the League Actually Runs

We did a precision pass on deadzones across the league. The standard everyone quotes isn't the standard.

Ask what CDL pros run for deadzones and you get one answer: 1/75 left, 3/99 right. We went through all 45 tracked players value by value. That answer is right more often than it is wrong — but calling it the standard hides where the real variation lives.

Start with what is actually fixed. Every single tracked player in the league runs a right stick maximum of 99. Forty-five out of forty-five, no exceptions. That is the only deadzone value in Black Ops 7 with no dissent at all, and it is the least interesting one, because there is no competitive reason to cap your aim stick short of its full range.

The left stick minimum is where the folklore is wrong in the other direction. The common claim is that pros bottom the left stick out at 0. Ten of them do — Mercules, Shotzzy, aBeZy, HyDra, Neptune, 04, Simp, Exnid, Nastie, Lunarz — but 32 of 45 sit on 1. A minimum of 1 is the league norm by a wide margin, and 0 is a minority position taken almost entirely by players on new custom pads that have not developed any stick drift yet.

Left stick maximums are where rosters genuinely diverge. Thirty-five players sit on 75, but the tail is long: Alluka caps at 60, Mamba at 63, Sib and Nium at 65, aBeZy, Exnid, Kips and Lunarz at 70. A lower maximum means full movement input registers before the stick physically reaches the edge of its travel, which is a preference about how much of the stick's range you want to spend.

One player sits entirely outside that pattern. Ghosty runs a left maximum of 99 — effectively no outer deadzone on the movement stick at all. He is the only tracked player in the league doing it.

Right stick minimums cluster harder than the left, with 34 of 45 on 3. The spread runs from 1 (Alluka, Kremp) up to 6, and the top of that range belongs to Mamba alone. He also carries a left minimum of 3 and a left maximum of 63, which together make his the loosest deadzone profile tracked anywhere in the league — a configuration that usually points at a pad with real drift rather than a taste preference.

The takeaway has not changed, but it is worth stating precisely. Sensitivity and FOV cluster because they are choices about how the game feels. Deadzones do not cluster in the same way because they are partly a measurement of a physical object — the specific controller a player is holding, and how worn its sticks are. Copying a deadzone number off a pro sheet transfers a setting that was tuned to hardware you do not have. If you are going to copy one thing from this page, copy 3/99 on the right stick and then find your own left minimum by testing your own drift.
