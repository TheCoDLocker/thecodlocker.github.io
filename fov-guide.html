Choosing an FOV That Suits You

FOV is an easy setting to notice and a surprisingly easy one to overthink. You can see the difference as soon as you move the slider: the view opens up, but the player you’re aiming at takes up less of the screen.

What changes when you raise it?

A higher field of view shows more to either side. That can help you notice someone near the edge of the screen in a close fight. The tradeoff is that enemies at a distance look smaller, so they can be harder to pick out.

A lower value does the reverse. You get a closer-looking view of what’s ahead, with less visible around it. Neither end of the slider gives you both advantages.

What do the pros use?

The confirmed BO7 profiles here range from 98 to 107. Of the 45 recorded values, 43 fall between 98 and 105. That’s a useful place to start if you want to see how a pro-style setup feels.

Those numbers don’t tell us why each player chose them. Your screen size, seating distance and ability to see targets are better reasons to make a change than a small difference between two players’ profiles.

How to choose yours

Try around 100, then look at a familiar lane from the same position. Can you see a distant player clearly? Do you feel too zoomed in when someone moves past you up close? Move the slider a few points and compare.

Keep sensitivity the same while you do this. Changing FOV can change how fast your aim appears to move, even when the sensitivity value hasn’t changed. Give yourself time to adjust before changing both.

When you find a view that’s comfortable in close fights and at range, stick with it for a few sessions. There’s no need to match a pro’s FOV exactly.
