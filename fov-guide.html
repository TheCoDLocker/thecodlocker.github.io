FOV: Choosing a View That Suits Your Role

Settings guide5 min readBlack Ops 7 · 2026 season

Your FOV should reflect the gunfights you spend most of your time taking. Someone holding longer lanes with an AR has different priorities from someone entering hills and clearing rooms with an SMG.

Lower FOV makes distant enemies appear larger on screen, while higher FOV shows more of your surroundings. That’s the basic tradeoff: how clearly you can see what’s ahead versus how much you can see around it. Call of Duty’s settings guide explains the same relationship.

If you mainly play AR

It makes sense to start toward the lower end of the range. You’ll often be watching a longer lane, holding a cross or trying to pick someone off a headglitch. In those situations, making a small target easier to read can be more useful than seeing a little farther toward the edges of your screen. If you’re constantly leaning forward to make out the person you’re shooting, your FOV may be wider than you need.

If you mainly play SMG

There’s more reason to try a wider view. When you’re moving through buildings, breaking a hill or fighting around a doorway, an opponent can move out of the centre of your screen quickly. Seeing more around the immediate fight can help you follow what’s happening, especially when there’s a second player nearby.

Across the 45 confirmed profiles, 18 players use exactly 100 FOV. That’s 40% of the sample. The average is 101.2, and 43 of the 45 values fall between 98 and 105. Most of the variation happens within a few points.

Where to start

Starting points, not limits

  • AR focus: try around 98–100 if you want better visibility at distance.
  • SMG focus: start around 100 and test a few points higher if close fights feel cramped.

Those are starting suggestions based on the tradeoff, rather than measured limits for either role.

Check the difference while aiming down sights as well. ADS FOV changes how your chosen view carries into aiming: Affected bases the ADS view on your FOV setting, while Independent uses a separate value. Keep that setting the same when comparing two FOVs, otherwise you’re changing more than one part of the picture. Call of Duty’s ADS FOV explanation.

How to judge the change

Use familiar fights. Watch the same lane with your AR, or clear the same room with your SMG. Keep your sensitivity steady and see whether you can identify and follow enemies more comfortably. Your role gives you a reason to test a particular range; your actual visibility should decide whether you stay there.
