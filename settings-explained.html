Pro Settings, Explained

A pro’s settings page gives you the numbers. Knowing what those numbers change makes it much easier to decide which ones to try. Here’s a quick guide to the settings you’ll see on CODLOCKER.

Sensitivity

Sensitivity controls how quickly you turn when you move the right stick. A higher value lets you turn faster with the same input; a lower one gives you more room for small adjustments.

In the BO7 records on this site, sensitivity runs from 1.60 to 2.10, with 40 of the 45 confirmed profiles between 1.65 and 1.85. That gives you a useful starting range, but the most popular number won’t automatically suit you. If you keep aiming past people, try a small reduction. If you struggle to turn far enough in close fights, try a small increase. Give each change a few sessions before deciding.

Some players use different horizontal and vertical values. Those control left-to-right and up-and-down aiming separately; they aren’t settings for the left and right sticks.

Field of view

FOV changes how much you can see on screen. Raising it shows more of your surroundings, while enemies look smaller at the same distance. Lowering it makes those enemies easier to see, but leaves less visible around the edges.

The recorded range is 98–107, with most players between 98 and 105. Start around 100 if you’re unsure, then check whether you can comfortably see people at range. Your screen size and how close you sit both matter. The FOV guide goes into more detail.

Deadzones

The minimum is how far a stick has to move before the game responds. Set it too high and small inputs get ignored. Set it too low for your controller and the game may register movement when you aren’t touching the stick.

The maximum is the point where the game reads full input. A lower left-stick maximum lets you reach full movement input without pushing the stick as far. It doesn’t increase your character’s top speed.

You’ll often see 1/75 on the left and 3/99 on the right in the database. Use those as a reference, then check for drift on your own pad. Our deadzone guide explains how to tune both sticks.

Response curve and aim assist

The response curve changes how turn speed builds as you push the stick farther. Dynamic is the curve recorded throughout the confirmed BO7 profiles here, alongside Default aim assist. They’re useful starting choices if you want to try a familiar pro setup.

These are separate settings: the curve changes your stick response, while aim assist affects aiming around targets. Choosing Dynamic doesn’t mean you’ve increased aim-assist strength. See response curves and aim assist for the distinction.

Button layout and grip

Default, Tactical, flipped layouts, paddles and claw all show up in pro setups. The practical question is whether you can jump, crouch or slide while keeping control of your aim.

If your current layout already lets you do that comfortably, you don’t need to change it because a favourite player uses something else. Try one change at a time so you can tell whether it helps.
