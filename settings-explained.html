Pro Settings, Explained

Every setting on a pro's page changes how the game feels, but they don't all matter equally. This guide explains what each one actually does, what the league has collectively settled on, and what's worth copying versus what's personal preference.

Sensitivity

Sensitivity controls how fast your view rotates when you move the right stick. Across the CDL the tracked field runs 1.60 to 2.10, but 40 of the 45 confirmed profiles sit inside 1.65–1.85, a far tighter band than the settings menu allows and lower than most casual players run. The reason is consistency: lower sens makes micro-adjustments repeatable, and pros win fights on centering and accuracy, not flick speed. Role is part of what pulls players either side of the middle. AR players tend to sit lower, because their gunfights happen at range and too much stick speed makes a long shot hard to hold. SMG players tend to sit a little higher, because they have to turn and reposition in close quarters and long-range precision matters less to them. That is a general trend and not much more: the two fastest players in the tracked field sit far above anything role alone would predict, and teammates in the same seat often run different numbers. A small number of players split their horizontal and vertical values, keeping vertical slightly different to fine-tune recoil control. If you take one thing from the pros, it's this: pick something in that 1.65–1.85 band and stop changing it.

FOV (Field of View)

FOV sets how much of the world fits on your screen. Higher values show more of your surroundings but make targets smaller; lower values zoom you in. The league runs 98 to 107, with all but two players inside 98–105, balancing peripheral information against target size. Players on the wider end are more often the ones fighting close on tight angles, where seeing what is in front of you beats seeing it clearly at distance. Players on the tighter end more often hold range, where a narrower view makes an enemy read larger on screen. The site does not track roles, so treat that as background on why the band is shaped the way it is rather than a claim about any individual player. Unlike sensitivity, FOV is genuinely personal — copy a pro's number as a starting point, then adjust to what your eyes prefer.

Deadzones

Deadzone minimums decide how far a stick must move before the game registers input; maximums decide where input caps out. Lower minimums mean faster response but risk stick drift on worn controllers. The de facto league standard is 1/75 on the left stick and 3/99 on the right, and you'll see it on page after page across the site. Both halves of that are deliberate. The left maximum of 75 means full movement input lands before the stick reaches the edge, so top speed arrives sooner, which matters in a game where movement decides so many fights. The right minimum of 3 is the lowest most players trust: it keeps fine aim control without letting the stick wander on its own. A handful tune either side of it, and one or two go lower deliberately, taking the extra control and living with a bit of drift. If your controller is new and drift-free, low minimums are free responsiveness; if you're getting phantom movement, raise the minimum until it stops.

Response curve and aim assist

This one's simple: the league runs Dynamic response curve and Default aim assist, essentially without exception. Dynamic front-loads stick response for faster initial aim while keeping fine control near center. There is no secret setting here — the pros use what the game gives everyone.

Button layouts and grip

Layouts vary more than any other setting — Default, Tactical, flipped variants, and one lone Bumper Jumper holdout are all represented, per the league's own published data. Tactical (crouch on the right stick) exists so players can slide and drop-shot without leaving the thumbstick, which is also why claw grip is common: it keeps aim and face buttons available at the same time. Many pros sidestep the tradeoff entirely with back paddles on custom controllers. This is the most personal category on the site — copy the concept (never leave your aim stick to press a button), not necessarily the exact layout.
